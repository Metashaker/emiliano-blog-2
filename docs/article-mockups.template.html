From Patterns to Programs

What a decade of cutting fabric taught me about cutting scope — and why the best engineers I know think like tailors.

Emiliano August 14, 2026 7 min read

For a decade my instruments were shears, tailor's chalk, and a dress form the exact shape of a stranger. A garment is not decoration; it is a system — the constraints of a body, a cloth, and a movement resolved into a single object that has to hold together when someone reaches for a door. When I started writing software, the tools changed and almost nothing else did.

A pattern, I realized, is an interface. A seam is a contract. And a bad fit, like a bad abstraction, announces itself the instant someone tries to move.

01The body is the spec

The blank canvas is a myth sold to people who have never shipped anything. In fashion, the body is the specification — non-negotiable, particular, indifferent to your taste. In software, the problem is. The discipline is identical: you listen to the constraint before you cut, because every cut you make in ignorance you pay for twice in alteration.

02Seams are contracts

Where two panels meet, a seam decides whether the whole thing moves or tears. Software has the same joints — the places where two modules meet and agree on what they owe each other. Sloppy there and it holds only until real force arrives, which in both trades means: a real person, doing a real thing, at the worst possible moment.

Elegance is not the absence of seams. It is seams placed exactly where the body bends.

You cannot hide a seam. You can only choose where it goes — and the choice, made well, disappears into the way the thing moves. That is the whole job, in cloth or in code.

03Measure twice

Every tailor learns the arithmetic of waste. Cut greedily and you run out of cloth on the last panel; cut with a plan and the same yardage yields the garment and a pocket square besides. Estimating engineering work is the identical muscle: the cost is set long before you touch the material.

04The long game

A good coat outlives the season it was made for; good software outlives the sprint. Both reward the same unglamorous virtues — restraint, legible structure, respect for whoever has to alter it next. I changed careers and kept the craft. This blog is where I write down what carried over.

Make it hold when someone reaches for the door.
